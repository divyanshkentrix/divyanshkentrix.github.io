Reading the map

What the geography adds to the ranking.

The network is a southern one

37 of the 54 outlets sit in the Southern region, concentrated along the Andhra Pradesh, Telangana and Tamil Nadu corridors. Fourteen are in the West, and only three across the North and East combined.

The strongest sites are not clustered

The ten largest outlets by addressable potential are spread across seven states rather than sitting on one corridor, so a first phase built on addressable potential alone would mean ten separate mobilisations. Grouping two or three geographically adjacent sites into a single phase may cost less per outlet than following the ranking strictly.

What the numbers mean

Read this before quoting a figure.

Addressable potential

The monthly non-fuel retail spend available at that location for a given format, in rupees per month. Six formats are scored: QSR – Burger, Café – Coffee, QSR – Pizza, Café – Chai, QSR – Fried Chicken and Convenience Store. Convenience Store and Café – Chai are modelled only at the 7 city and periphery outlets, where a resident catchment supports them. It is the size of the opportunity at the site — the spend all operators there could take between them — not a revenue forecast for a BPCL store.

BPCL capture assumption

The share of that addressable spend a BPCL outlet would take. It is set in the bar at the top of the dashboard and defaults to 30%; every BPCL revenue figure moves with it. This is a planning input for BPCL to set, not a Kentrix output — vary it to see how the case holds at different share levels.

Recommended format

The format with the highest modelled potential at that outlet. The formats are alternatives for the same site, not additions — a two-format pad competes for the same demand.

Rank

The network rank orders all 54 outlets by best-format potential. The rank beside each format orders only the outlets scored on that format; Convenience Store and Café – Chai are scored at 7 outlets, so their ranks run 1–7.

Where the data comes from

Kentrix Geomarketeer definitions.

Footfall and unique visitor index

Kentrix indexes the footfall a location can attract, including repeat visits, against its retail propensity model for the store format and city tier. Both figures here sit on one scale where 100 is the median outlet's weekly footfall, so an outlet at 200 draws twice the median, and footfall index divided by unique visitor index gives the repeat rate shown alongside. Unique visitors de-duplicate repeat visits. Sources are anonymised mobile location data, transaction-based retail data and travel bookings.

Income profile of traffic

Footfall is split across Kentrix's eight economic tiers, each defined by monthly household income: Low (under ₹20k), Medium Low (₹20k–40k), Medium (₹40k–80k), Medium Good (₹80k–1.3 L), Good (₹1.3–1.7 L), Very Good (₹1.7–2.3 L), High (₹2.3–3 L) and Very High (above ₹3 L). Summaries in this deck group them as above ₹1.7 L, ₹80k–1.7 L and below ₹80k.

Corridor toll traffic

Vehicle volume on the corridor serving the outlet, as recorded in the source data, with an index against the network median alongside it. It describes the road the site sits on rather than traffic measured at the forecourt.

Catchment

Resident data is aggregated inside a drive-time isochrone on the road network. Population and household counts are built from building-level records; income is a weighted average across observed bands.

Lifestyle segments

Kentrix's Lifestyle Segmentation Index classifies households into 12 archetypes from brand-purchase behaviour across 40+ categories, together with education, occupation, income, travel and financial holdings. Charts show only the segments actually present at a site. In descending affluence the archetypes run: Established Elite, Affluent New Wealth, Aspiring Middle Class, Conservative Middle Class, Successful Runner-Ups, Upcoming Climbers, Struggling Climbers and Trailers (the lowest urban discretionary-spend group), plus Dependants — children, homemakers, students and retirees who live in the household but do not earn.

Two site types, two evidence bases

Which fields appear on an outlet profile depends on the type of site.

Highway outlets are scored on transit demand: modelled footfall and its income composition, the roadside amenity base within 5 km and connectivity to nearby cities. Sales there are stop-and-go, which makes forecourt access, signage and service turnaround the operating variables that decide the outcome.

City and periphery outlets are scored on resident catchment and the structure of the micro-market inside 500 m. Demand there is habitual and local, so trading hours, delivery-aggregator presence and a repeat-visit menu matter more than throughput.

Each profile shows the fields its own site type supports.
